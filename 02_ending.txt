THE TOWER, RECONCILIATION, HOMECOMING AND POST-CREDITS

PLAYABLE POV: DRACONIAN RIDER
The Draconian crosses the land to the great tower where four riders and dragons once lived; now three remain. He climbs to the human quarters at the tower's upper level and confronts the human red rider who betrayed him. The red rider mocks him, saying the others won't believe him because he already told them what “really happened.” The Draconian draws his repaired sword and challenges him: “Face me man to man, you coward.” The red rider shouts, drawing the green-clad and tan-clad riders down from the stairwell. The green rider reaches for his sword, but the tan rider restrains him and asks the Draconian what he is doing. After a brief exchange, all four riders fight. Badly wounded, the Draconian retreats up the stairs into the dragons' quarters, limping with his sword.

TOWER GEOGRAPHY
A central stairwell reaches human living quarters; a further stairwell reaches the dragon living quarters. From the central upper room, four personal dragon chambers connect to four outward projecting launch/landing perches. The dragons cannot descend the human stairwell to intervene in the riders' fight. The red dragon eventually flees by leaping from the PURPLE perch, not necessarily its own.

THE DRAGONS' JUDGMENT
The three dragons have been watching the stairwell and initially glare at the Draconian, nostrils smoking, believing him the traitor. He shows his deceased dragon's heart crystal and says he would never have betrayed them; the red dragon and its rider are the betrayers who lured him into the sky to be shot down. The other two dragons are shocked by the crystal and reconsider. The red dragon insists he is lying; the others question it. As the three other riders arrive upstairs, the green and tan dragons advance on the red dragon, backing it toward a landing perch. The red dragon attacks the green dragon's neck; the tan dragon strikes the red dragon's left wing with a fire bolt. The red dragon rolls to its feet, races toward its rider, who mounts while it runs, and jumps from the purple perch. The green and tan dragons each fire a bolt into its back, sending dragon and rider spiraling downward.

REUNION
The two surviving dragons ask the Draconian to show the heart crystal to their riders. Seeing its BLUE glow, the two riders know he told the truth. They put away their weapons and embrace him as their brother, grieving with him. He says: “The grief that I feel could never be taken away by a single moment or even replaced by any amount of time. But there is one thing for which I am joyful for, that I am here again with you my brothers. Though I may bear pain and I may have grief I do see light, there's a joy waiting for me expectant of my return.” He entrusts his companion's crystal to his brothers so they can care for it and ensure it is properly reborn. His grief remains; joy and love coexist with it.

FUNERAL AND RETURN
The next day they hold a water funeral for the red dragon and its rider. The rider's body is shrouded and ignited as it drifts on the water; the dragon is sent into the water in the dragon tradition. Three days later the Draconian returns to his new home in town. The healer runs to him with open arms, embraces him and says, “You were gone too long.” He replies, “But now I'm here to stay.” He greets the apprentice. Together they walk into town and disappear into the lantern-lit, sparkling night square. Credits.

POST-CREDITS
The camera looks out over the waters where the red rider and dragon were buried, then descends underwater. A RED heart crystal slowly pulses and begins changing to PURPLE. Cut away. This hints at the dragon's eventual spiritual renewal without explaining it.

THEMATIC NOTE
The Draconian sets out desiring revenge, but the final deaths result from the dragons' confrontation, not a simple successful sword execution. Do not equate the deaths with the disappearance of his grief. The healer's self-sacrificial love includes allowing him to go when her town needs her.
